About this concept

Design question

Can a separate visualization layer explain a selected object's relationships better than lines drawn into the tree? The tree keeps physical containment only. Selecting an entry makes it the focus of a compass whose bridges sort the same relationship records by direction and by whether they cross a perimeter.

Reading the compass

Perimeter
The dashed frame around the focus. Nearest bundle uses the closest enclosing bundle, or the focus itself when it is a bundle. Application uses Atlas.app. Reference records lie outside the inspected tree.
North · ingress
Relationships that reach the focus from the other side of the perimeter. They enter through the north gate.
West · internal ingress
References to the focus from the same side of the perimeter.
East · internal resolution
References from the focus that resolve, or are proposed to resolve, without leaving the perimeter. Runpaths and version links appear as resolution steps.
South · egress
References from the focus to targets on the other side. They leave through the south gate.
Unplaced
Endpoints with unknown containment. They are neither inside nor outside, so they are not routed through a gate.
Lines
Traffic reads top to bottom and left to right. Blue reaches the focus; amber leaves it. Solid: recorded. Dashed: derived or candidate. Dotted: string or inferred evidence.

Changing the perimeter reclassifies the same records; it never adds, removes or upgrades evidence. A directory or bundle focus bridges only relationships that cross its own boundary. Relationships wholly inside it are counted in the focus details.

Compared with the mixed relation overlay

  • The tree carries no overlay, so expansion, scrolling and collapsed ancestors never hide or approximate an endpoint. Each bridge card names its exact endpoint.
  • The perimeter makes relocation hazards visible: an absolute reference that leaves a staged bundle while a contained copy exists is marked as a bypass.
  • Trade-off: an endpoint's position in the tree is not visible at a glance. Locate in tree reveals it on request without changing the focus.
  • Open questions: how a bridge summarizes hundreds of system references, whether a perimeter should follow signing units instead of bundles, and whether relationships inside a container need their own view.

Evidence and scope limits

  • Files, relationships and statuses are fixed illustrative data created for the design conversation, not inspection results. The tree represents a staged copy of Atlas.app.
  • A recorded load name is not a loaded library; a candidate is not a resolved target; a seal entry or bound hash is not integrity or trust; an entitlement is not observed activity.
  • Incoming coverage is limited to the example's records. Consumers elsewhere on the system are not inventoried.
  • Bridge classification and the bypass, stand-in and provider notes are computed by this page's JavaScript for discussion only. In a product they would be relationship semantics composed by their ordinary Rust owners and only presented by the UI. No Rust analyzer, WIT component, filesystem protocol, native selection, resolver, signer or tracing backend is involved.
